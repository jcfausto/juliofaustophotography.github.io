I passed by this building on many walks before I decided to photograph it more seriously. I was always fascinated by how it sits on the riverbank, by its shapes, and especially by its roof, which, depending on where you stand, can look like a hat or a pair of wings. The photographs in this essay set out to show not only what the building looks like, but also to tell its story and capture what its architect meant it to represent.

Stubbins wanted the building to be seen as a symbol of freedom, and this intention is reflected in the roof, which mimics a pair of wings. Initially, the roof was meant to stand on only two supports, but this proved unfeasible. A concrete cylinder was therefore added to hold it up. Berliners, less poetic than Stubbins, saw something else in the roof and nicknamed the building "Die schwangere Auster" ("The pregnant oyster").

The wings came at a price. On 21 May 1980, part of the southern roof collapsed, killing a journalist and injuring several others. The investigation found that moisture had corroded the steel cables inside the concrete, the very structure that let the roof appear to float. The hall was rebuilt in its original form and reopened in 1987. Standing under the roof today, it is hard to see where the old building ends and the new one begins, and that is part of what I tried to photograph: a shape that still promises flight while it rests on everything that was added to hold it up.
